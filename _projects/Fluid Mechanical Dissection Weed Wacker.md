## Overview
In MAE 3230 (Fluid Mechanics), students are exposed to a wide range of fluid mechanical systems. To conclude the course, the final project involved disassembling and analyzing a real mechanical system in order to identify how fluid mechanics principles enable its operation. Each group was tasked with producing a five-minute technical video explaining the system through the lens of fluid mechanics.

Our group was assigned a two-stroke weed trimmer (weed wacker). Through physical inspection and analysis, we identified several key fluid mechanical components, including a centrifugal fan, a Venturi carburetor, internal flow passages and piping, and airfoil-like geometries. In addition, we examined supporting mechanical and thermodynamic components, such as the piston–crankshaft mechanism, two-stroke combustion cycle, and a cam-driven gear system.

Using manufacturer specifications along with justified engineering assumptions, we estimated critical performance quantities to support our analysis. These calculations were used to connect theoretical fluid mechanics concepts—such as conservation of mass and energy, Bernoulli’s principle, and turbomachinery fundamentals—to the behavior of the real system. This approach allowed us to demonstrate both conceptual understanding and practical application of fluid mechanics in an integrated electromechanical system.

### Individual Contribution
My primary contribution focused on the fluid mechanics of the centrifugal blower and volute. I analyzed how intentional geometric design choices, including logarithmic and Archimedean spiral profiles, influence flow guidance, velocity distribution, and pressure rise. I also evaluated velocity components, volute expansion, and impeller–volute interaction to explain how the blower efficiently moves air through the system.
